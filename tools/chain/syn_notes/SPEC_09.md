## State at entry
The factory (the Street program, or a Blender pipeline per Lesson 8) emits frames, masks, labels. Nothing in it is looked at. The exam of Lesson 1 and the contract of Lessons 7–8 are about *what* the data means; this lesson is about *whether the data is what the pipeline says it is*, every time it runs.

## The one new idea
**An unattended factory must carry its own evidence: a manifest that makes every frame a replayable function of (code, config, seed), invariants derived from the contract that every frame and every batch must satisfy, and splits made by scene lineage so that nothing the exam scores was already seen.** Each answers a different failure — drift, silent corruption, self-scoring — and each failure has a measurable detection probability.

## The argument you must derive
1. **Why looking fails** (arithmetic): a corruption touching a fraction f of the frames is seen by an inspector who looks at k frames with probability 1 − (1 − f)ᵏ: tabulate f = 10%, 1%, 0.1% and k = 20, 100, 1,000; at a million frames and a 0.1% corruption a hundred-frame spot check finds it 10% of the time. Looking is a bad *detector*; it is a good *debugger*. So the checks must be code, run on **every** frame.
2. **Replay.** In this lab a frame is a pure function of (code version, config, seeds): derive what a manifest row must hold so that a frame can be re-made bit-exactly (config hash, the three seeds of `SV.sample`, code version, the split, a checksum of the pixels and one of the labels), its size per frame, and the *replay audit*: re-render a random sample of rows and compare checksums. What replay **catches** (code or dependency drift, nondeterminism, a frame edited after the fact) and **cannot** (a bug that is deterministic and was there on the first run). Show both with injected faults.
3. **Invariants** (the contract of Lessons 7–8 turned into checks), each a pure function of a frame record and each with a stated tolerance *derived from the lab's own numerics* (not tuned on the bugs): e.g. pixel range and non-degeneracy; ground-plane law on the depth map (z = f·hc/(v + ½ − V0) on every ground pixel); range = depth·√(1 + tan²φ + ρ²); label rule vs the mask's area; pedestrian size law (mask height ≈ f·H/z at its depth); horizon row; sky chromaticity against the declared sky; **noise law on a flat patch** (the calibration of Lesson 3); logged gain within [1, maxGain]; batch-level: pedestrian rate within the binomial interval of the declared pPed, no duplicate frame hashes across splits, seed blocks disjoint. **Measure the false-alarm rate of each invariant on ≥ 5,000 clean frames (must be 0 at your tolerance)**, then
4. **The silent-bug bench**: inject m ≈ 12 plausible faults (list in your notes; examples: image flipped but not the mask; R and B swapped; gamma applied twice; depth returned as range; focal length wrong in the renderer only; mask shifted by one pixel; label threshold off; a seed reused between train and test for 30% of frames; a stale cache repeating 5% of frames; one batch with rows reversed; horizon off by one row; read noise missing in one batch; pixel centres at +0 instead of +½). For each fault × invariant record caught / not caught, and the **number of frames k an invariant needs to catch it with probability 0.95** when the fault touches a fraction f (closed form 1 − (1 − f·c)ᵏ with c the invariant's per-frame detection probability; measured c). Count how many faults the invariants catch (⟦k⟧ of m).
5. **The canary**: a cheap *training* check each run (N = 160, ≈ 2 s) graded on a fixed small held-out set from the street (spent once, reused for every run: say what that costs) — which of the faults that invariants missed does it catch, at what threshold, with what false-alarm rate over clean runs? The union: ⟦k⟧ of m caught by invariants, ⟦k′⟧ by the canary, the remainder only by the exam or never. Be honest about the never.
6. **Leaks: the exam scoring the data against itself.** A factory that renders each scene under several lights and noise draws (siblings; `SV.stream(seed, 'scene')` shared, look and sensor streams different) and then splits *frames* at random puts siblings on both sides of the split. A **memorising learner** (1-nearest-neighbour on a fixed feature vector; also k = 10) scores a held-out set it has effectively seen; the fixed detector (a small linear head: very little capacity to memorise) barely does. Measure held-out AUC / miss for random split vs **split by lineage** (group = scene seed) for 1-NN, 10-NN and the detector: the gap is the leak, and it **grows with capacity**. Add the seed-reuse leak of the bench (identical frames in train and test: 0% miss on a detector that has not learned anything, e.g. a memoriser) and the check that finds it (a hash join across splits). Note, without an experiment, the same effect at corpus scale (FACTS: Barz & Denzler 2020 near-duplicates in CIFAR; give the verified numbers only).
7. **Exit, demonstrated**: the program `aaaa` (the naive one of Lessons 1–2) **passes every invariant, the replay audit and the lineage split** — its frames are exactly what the pipeline says they are — and its detector misses 80% on the street. The checks establish that the number describes *the program*; they cannot establish that the program describes the street. That is the baton: only frames from the street can say how wrong.

## What you implement (private `l09_factory.js`, namespace `SV.l09`)
`manifestRow(pipe, seed)`, `makeManifest`, `checksum` (FNV-1a over quantised values, node and browser identical), `replay(manifest, k)`; the invariants as pure functions on records returned by `SV.sample` (documented tolerances); the bug injectors (each wraps the generator or the data path; **do not change street.js**: build records from `SV.sample`/`SV.render`/`SV.sense`, then corrupt them); the canary; the siblings generator; 1-NN/k-NN on a fixed low-dimensional feature (document it; keep it fast: ≤ 3 s per evaluation).

## Measurements and the builder (small: ≤ 30 trainings, most with N = 160–800)
False-alarm table of invariants on clean frames; the fault × check table; the detection-probability vs k curves (closed form + Monte Carlo); the canary's clean-run distribution (≥ 30 runs of N = 160) and its response to each missed fault; the leak table (3 learners × 2 splits × 3 seeds); the `aaaa`-passes-everything line (cheap: reuse `SV.TABLES`).

## Widget (suggestion)
Main slider = **frames checked k** (1 … 1,000, log). A select picks a fault (and "none"). The page generates k frames live (deterministic seeds), injects the fault, runs every invariant, and shows: the grid of invariants (green/red counts), the fraction of frames flagged, the closed-form detection probability 1 − (1 − f c)ᵏ beside the observed one, and one frame before/after the corruption. A second panel: random vs lineage split leak numbers for the chosen learner (from the table).

## Oracle must check
The checksum's exactness and sensitivity; replay finds the drift fault and misses a deterministic one; each invariant's clean false-alarm rate; closed-form detection probabilities against Monte Carlo; the bug bench table recomputed for ≥ 3 faults from scratch; the leak gap on one learner from scratch; `aaaa` passes everything; widget states.

## Do not
Say a check is "complete"; do not claim the bench's coverage generalises beyond the faults listed. No statistical tests you do not need (a binomial interval and a hash join are enough). Do not cover real-frame budgets (Lesson 10) or consequences (Lesson 11). Companion reads: `../data_engineering/index.html` if it exists (check), `../computer_vision/index.html`.
