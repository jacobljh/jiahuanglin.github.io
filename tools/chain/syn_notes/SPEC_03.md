## State at entry (what the reader knows from Lessons 1–2)
The program draws a pedestrian's picture exactly as the renderer's radiance, quantised: an *ideal sensor* (`SV.SIM0.sensor.ideal = true`, fixed exposure, no noise, no blur). The street's camera is not ideal. Swapping only the camera takes the miss rate from 80.3% to 65.8% (`aaba`), a price of 16.3 points,
49% of the gap, and the label-free meter hardly moves (100% → 93.7% of unlabelled frames alarm). The evidence that sees the camera needs no labels and no street: *a grey card, an edge, the exposure log*.

## The one new idea
**A pixel is a measurement: a signal plus noise with a law whose few parameters can be read off scene-free evidence.** (Photon shot noise + read noise: variance = a·mean + b; plus a blur and an exposure controller.) You can calibrate the camera from an afternoon of flat frames, one edge and a day of exposure logs — and the calibration is a *program*, not a picture.

## The argument you must derive (each step forced by the one before; reject alternatives by computation)
1. **What is wrong with the ideal sensor** (feel it): the same scene and light through the ideal sensor and through the street's camera (re-sense one radiance image both ways: `SV.sample`'s streams make this possible; replicate its six lines, it returns only the sensed image). Show a pair; show what a detector trained on the first does on the second (the table says so; one live score is enough). Pose the question as a measurement problem: *what is the distribution of the pixel value given the radiance?*
2. **The noise law, by elimination.** Flat frames of a uniform patch at a ladder of exposures (manual mode: no auto-exposure; exposure doubling gives *exact* level ratios — the camera's own dial is the ruler). Hypotheses: (a) no noise; (b) constant (additive) noise; (c) noise ∝ level (multiplicative); (d) variance = a·mean + b. Compute the residuals of each against the measured (mean, variance) pairs and let the numbers rule them out; then *derive* (d) from physics (Poisson arrivals: variance = mean in electrons; read noise adds a constant) and read the parameters: in linear units var(q) = mean(q)/fw + (r/fw)², so slope = 1/fw gives the **full well** and the intercept gives the **read noise** — in absolute electrons, with no knowledge of the scene. Check the saturation knee gives the same fw. (EMVA-1288-style two-frame differences remove fixed-pattern noise; use them if it simplifies; the lab has none.)
3. **Gamma and the quantiser, before the noise can be read**: DN is not linear in light; the exposure ladder gives γ from the log-log slope (ratios are exact). State what quantisation adds and when it matters (low levels), and keep the fit away from the clipped ends (or model them).
4. **Blur from an edge** by analysis-by-synthesis (the program's own renderer already integrates each pixel over its area: calibrate the *difference*, not the absolute width): image an edge target, fit the program's edge response to the camera's, read σ. State the 10–90% width ↔ σ constant only if FACTS verifies it, else derive it.
5. **The exposure controller from the log**: per-frame metadata gain (`SV.evidence.logs(n)` → `{x, gain}`) plus the frame's mean linear level give the law gain = clamp(target/level, 1, maxGain): the *target* is where the gain leaves 1, *maxGain* is the plateau; show the cap is reached by **a third of the street's frames** (33.5% of `SV.evidence.logs(200)` sit at gain 8: say what you measure on 1,000) — dark frames are noisy frames, which is why the camera costs so much.
6. **Assemble and price**: the calibrated sensor stage (all estimates, with error bars as a function of the amount of evidence: frames per level 1…32; logs 10…1000) → `aaba`-like program; **exam it against the exact-stage `aaba` (65.8%) and `aaaa` (80.3%)**; and price the camera's *parts* with the lab's privilege (noise / blur / auto-exposure switched on one at a time and in pairs; ≥ 8 programs × 3 seeds): which part of the 16-point price is which? Report what the lab shows, including a part that is worth nothing.
7. **Which pedestrians does noise erase?** Contrast-to-noise ratio of a pedestrian from the calibrated noise law (at the gain the camera logged) against the miss rate — only if the lab shows a clean relation (a Rose-style criterion if FACTS verifies it); if it does not, say so in one honest paragraph and show the rule of thumb the data supports instead.
8. **Road not taken · motion.** The camera also integrates over time: image-plane blur = f·v·t/Z (derive). Compute it for a pedestrian stepping out at 1.5 m/s at 8 m and for the shuttle's own motion at t = 10 ms for this 96-px camera (f = 83.1 px) and for a 1,920-px camera (f = 1,662 px): sub-pixel here, several pixels there. Resolution decides whether time is in the program; here it is not. (One short section; verify the formula by brute force: integrate a moving edge over the exposure.)
9. **Exit** (demonstrated with a number): the calibrated program's frames have one brightness; the street's do not. Show the **distribution of the logged auto-exposure gain** for street frames (1.9–8, median ≈ 6, a third at the cap) against the same quantity for the program's frames (≈ 1.6 and all alike): the exposure log is itself a light meter, and it says the street is darker and far more varied than the world the program draws. The meter (alarm rate on unlabelled frames) is still **93.7%** for `aaba`. That is the baton.

## Evidence you implement (private `l03_camera.js`, namespace `SV.l03`)
* `flats(stops, n, seed0)`: for each exposure setting (stops relative to a card of unit radiance), `n` frames of a uniform patch through the street's camera in **manual mode** (gain 1, auto-exposure off), as `Float32Array` DN images. Cost: an afternoon with the camera.
* `edge(n, seed0)`: frames of a black/white step target at random sub-pixel positions (manual mode). Cost: one printed card.
* the exposure log: `SV.evidence.logs(n)` (shared). A *light-level scale* is declared, not measured: a card of unit radiance under the program's unit light (say so).
* the estimators (`fitPhotonTransfer`, `fitGamma`, `fitEdge`, `fitExposure`), pure functions of those frames only; the **program-side** sensor with switches (noise / blur / auto-exposure / the calibrated parameter set) for the ablations (copy `SV.sense`'s logic; do not change it).
