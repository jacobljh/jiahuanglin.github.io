## State at entry
The contract of Lesson 7 is a list of clauses settled in `street.js` (axes, units, intrinsics, pixel centres, depth vs range, masks, coverage resolution, the camera model of Lesson 3 kept *outside* the renderer: `SV.sense`). A team does not draw its own frames: it builds scenes in a renderer it did not write. Blender 5.2.2 LTS is installed here; defaults I measured with `tools/chain/syn_notes/proto/blender/probe1.py` and `probe2.py`:
engine `BLENDER_EEVEE` by default (Cycles settable, `sc.render.engine = 'CYCLES'`); view transform **AgX**; Cycles pixel filter **Blackman-Harris 1.5 px**; camera lens **50 mm**, sensor **36 mm**, fit AUTO; units metric, scale 1; resolution 1920 × 1080; image colour depth 8. A 96 × 24 Cycles render takes ≈ 0.02 s plus 0.5 s start-up; an EEVEE first render ≈ 2.5 s.
FACTS L08 is binding for every Blender statement (it was produced by running this build; its traps list in §0 too: e.g. depth is the distance along the camera axis, mist uses the ray distance; `media_type = 'MULTI_LAYER_IMAGE'` is needed before `OPEN_EXR_MULTILAYER`; `shift_y` is in units of the fit side and positive moves the picture *down*; the box pixel filter ignored its width in the test; Cycles is bit-reproducible on a fixed build/device/settings with adaptive sampling and denoising off, independent of thread count; EXR metadata carries timestamps, so hash decoded pixels, not file bytes).
Also measured: a camera at height 1.4 m looking along +world Y has `rotation_euler = (π/2, 0, 0)`; with `sensor_fit = 'HORIZONTAL'` and `lens = 83.1·36/96 mm` the focal length is 83.1 px; **`shift_y = −3/96` puts the horizon on row 9** (a negative shift moves the picture *up*; shifts are in units of the larger image side); Python-side pixel arrays (`image.pixels`) run **bottom row first**.

## The one new idea
**A renderer obeys a contract only if each clause is set explicitly and then tested against an analytic truth — the defaults are someone else's contract, and a violated clause produces plausible pictures.** Test clause by clause with scenes whose correct answer is known in closed form (a plane at a known distance, an edge at a known position, a patch of known radiance, a cylinder at a known place), then compare whole frames with the Street renderer, and finally train on the production frames and take the exam.

## The argument you must derive
1. **Feel the entry problem** with a measured picture: the same scene (exported from the Street as a description: parts with kind, x, z, radius or half-extents, y0, y1, and colours) built in Blender with every setting at its default, next to the Street's frame. Something is plainly different (lens, horizon, colours); show the error map and one number. Pose it as: *which differences are conventions and which are bugs, and how do I know when I am done?*
2. **The rule for knowing when you are done**: a *conformance suite* of analytic scenes, each testing one clause and returning a number that is zero when the clause holds; plus **rendering radiance, not pictures** (float EXR/linear, view transform irrelevant; the sensor of Lesson 3 stays ours: `SV.sense` takes Blender's radiance), plus **masks by construction**: the pedestrian's visible mask = alpha of a render where every other object is a holdout (modal), the silhouette = alpha of the pedestrian alone (amodal); shading by emission shaders computing the Street's radiance formula from the geometry normal (so only conventions, not lighting models, differ).
3. **The conventions, one test each** (m ≈ 10–12; for each: the default's measured error, the setting that fixes it, the measured error after): axes and handedness (a post at x = +2 appears right of centre), lens and sensor fit (projected width of a known post → focal length in px), principal point / horizon row (`shift_y`), pixel centres and image row order (an edge placed at a known half-pixel; the bottom-row-first pixel array), depth pass semantics (depth along the axis vs range along the ray: compare with the ground-plane law z = f·hc/(v − V0) and with the range formula), object-index masks vs alpha masks at edges (FACTS L08, checked on 5.2.2: Cycles' Object Index and Depth are **point-sampled at the pixel centre** — hard labels with no coverage, exactly like the Street's centre-ray `depth`, `range` and `id`; EEVEE delivered no Object Index; Cryptomatte and the holdout-alpha construction give coverage: measure the area of a sliver under each), pixel filter type and width (edge-spread width: Blackman-Harris 1.5 px vs box 1.0 px: derive the extra σ and what it does to a sliver's area), colour management (the 8-bit view-transformed output vs the 32-bit scene-linear EXR: radiance of a known patch), sampling noise and **determinism** (same settings twice; changed seed; changed thread count if you can: say what you find), primitive conventions (the cylinder's `depth` is its full height and its origin its centre: the pedestrian floats; the 32-gon's radius error), units. Include conventions that were **fine at the default** (units; say which) — the honest count k of m is the baton.
4. **Close the loop with whole frames**: build the same ≥ 24 random Street scenes in Blender with all clauses set; per-pixel radiance error vs the Street renderer (mean, 99th percentile; what is left: polygonal cylinders, sampling noise); then the **path from defaults to conformance** as an ordered sequence of settings (the widget's main slider: "conventions set: 0 … m"; each step stored as a recorded render of the same two scenes).
5. **Train on the production frames**: 1,600 Blender frames (all settings) pushed through `SV.sense` of the street's camera (use the exact-stage program's light and scene, i.e. a `bbba`-like generator whose *renderer* is Blender) and labels from Blender's masks; the fixed detector; the exam, 3 seeds, against the Street-rendered `bbba` cell (46.8%). Then two defective variants (default pixel filter; default colour management or default lens, your choice by which hurts) at 1 seed each, to show a silent failure in *points*. Report what the lab shows.
6. **The cost side**: render throughput (frames per second, Cycles samples vs error), the EEVEE alternative in one measured paragraph (start-up, edge rule), and a manifest sketch: what must be recorded to replay a Blender frame (version, settings, seed, scene hash): the entry to Lesson 9.
7. **Exit** (demonstrated): the production pipeline now runs 1,600 frames with no human looking at a picture. Show one **silent corruption** that none of the clauses above would catch because it is in the *data path*, not the renderer — e.g. a file written with the wrong row order for one batch, a seed reused between two splits (identical frames in train and test), or a stale cache — and what it does to the exam (a number; e.g. the exam improves because the test set leaks). That is the baton: the checks must run on every frame, and the splits must be audited.

## What you implement
* `tools/syn_blender/`: `export_scene.js` (or a function in `l08_blender.js`: Street scene + look → JSON), `build_scene.py` (JSON → Blender scene with explicit settings, a `settings` dict whose keys are the conventions and whose values default to Blender's), `render_suite.py` (conformance scenes → arrays), `render_frames.py` (batch of scene JSONs → float32 radiance + masks, one Blender start-up per batch), README.md (commands). Output arrays as raw little-endian float32 `.bin` plus a JSON header (no EXR parser in node).
* `l08_blender.js` (browser + node): the Street-side comparison utilities, the closed-form expectations, decoding of recorded frames; `l08_data.js`: **recorded** frames (12-bit quantised, base64) for the slider's steps and the conformance results; the page *recomputes* the error maps and numbers from the recorded frames and the live Street renderer (so the widget computes, with Blender's output as recorded input — say so in the hint).
* The oracle: recompute every conformance number from the recorded arrays against closed forms; recompute the whole-frame errors with the Street renderer; `child_process` re-run of **one** conformance scene and one frame in Blender **if the binary exists** (skip with a note otherwise), requiring identical results; the exam cells from the builder (anchor 1–2 cells from scratch); widget states.
