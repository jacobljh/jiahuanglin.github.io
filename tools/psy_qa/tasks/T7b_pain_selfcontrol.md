## 10 · 痛不等于伤 (≈130 bullets)
Story: pain looks like a direct report of tissue damage; it is not. It is a protective output computed by the brain from nociceptive input plus context, expectation and threat estimate (a prediction); damage without pain and pain without damage both occur; this explains phantom limbs, imaging findings in people with no pain, placebo/nocebo, chronic pain that outlasts healing.
- ★ [def] IASP 2020 revised definition (Raja et al. 2020 *Pain* 161:1976–1982) and its six notes — exact wording (esp. "Pain and nociception are different phenomena. Pain cannot be inferred solely from activity in sensory neurons"). nociception vs pain; ICD-11 chronic primary pain / 3-month convention; nociplastic pain (IASP 2017).
- ★ Imaging findings in people WITHOUT pain: Brinjikji et al. 2015 *AJNR* 36:811–816 (33 studies, 3,110 asymptomatic individuals; prevalence of disc degeneration, disc bulge, protrusion, annular fissure by age decade — give the full age series, e.g. at 20/30/40/50/60/70/80); the companion Brinjikji 2015 *AJNR* (findings are MORE common in people with back pain — the odds ratios: the association is real but weak/non-specific); Jensen et al. 1994 *NEJM* (98 asymptomatic adults, 52% bulging disc at ≥1 level, etc.); Boden 1990; knee (Guermazi 2012 *BMJ* Framingham/MOST; Culvenor 2019 *BJSM*), shoulder (rotator-cuff tears in the pain-free: Minagawa 2013 / Sher 1995). Grade A/B each. NOTE: say precisely what it does and does NOT show (it does not show that scans are useless or pain unrelated to structure).
- ★ Pain without damage and damage without pain: congenital insensitivity to pain (Cox et al. 2006 *Nature* SCN9A; later reports; what happens to those people — injuries, shortened lifespans: cite a clinical series), phantom limb pain (prevalence Limakatso/Cashin/McAuley 2020 *PLoS One*? ≈ 64% — verify; Ramachandran), complex regional pain, pain after healing. Beecher 1946/1956 wounded soldiers ("75% … no medication") — find the primary source and what is wrong with the textbook version; grade C or [✗] as appropriate.
- ★ Expectation shapes pain: Wager et al. 2004 *Science* (placebo analgesia and anticipatory/pain-related fMRI; N, rating change), Atlas & Wager 2012 review / Atlas et al. 2010 *J Neurosci* (cue-based expectation → rated pain; effect sizes), Koyama et al. 2005 *PNAS* ("where expectations become reality"; N and rating change), Eippert et al. 2009 *Neuron* (naloxone blocks placebo analgesia; and the later complication), Colloca/Benedetti nocebo (hyperalgesia from negative suggestion), Vase/Riley/Price 2002 (lab placebo analgesia ES vs clinical-trial ES), Hróbjartsson & Gøtzsche 2010 (already in FACTS_T6a: pain SMD −0.28; point to it, don't redo). Give the sizes as published; grade B.
- ★ Threat and attention modulate pain: fear-avoidance model (Vlaeyen & Linton 2000/2012), pain catastrophizing (Quartana et al. 2009 *Expert Rev Neurother*; Sullivan 1995 scale), Villemure & Bushnell 2002 (mood), Crombez; social/context buffering exists (FACTS_T6c — point). Grade B/C.
- ★ Treatments built on this account (RESULTS ONLY, no advice): Pain Reprocessing Therapy — Ashar et al. 2022 *JAMA Psychiatry* RCT (N=151 chronic back pain; proportion pain-free/nearly pain-free post-treatment and at 1 year vs placebo injection and usual care; design caveats: unblinded, waitlist-like comparator, one trial, same research group) and any later replication (2023–2026); pain neuroscience education (Watson et al. 2019 *J Pain*? / Louw 2016 / Wood 2019?) — meta-analytic size; cognitive functional therapy RESTORE trial (Kent et al. 2023 *Lancet*); Cochrane psychological therapies for chronic pain (Williams, Fisher et al. 2020). Grade B/C and say which is single-trial. This section must be descriptive: "in this trial, X% vs Y%".
- Mirror therapy for phantom limb pain: Ramachandran & Rogers-Ramachandran 1996; Chan et al. 2007 *NEJM* (N=22); Cochrane (Barbin 2016 / Herrador Colmenero 2018?) verdict; grade C.
- Gate control (Melzack & Wall 1965 *Science*) — history; what survives of it ([def]/[V]); one sentence.
- The "pain is a prediction" framing: cite a reputable review (Büchel/Geuter/Sprenger/Eippert 2014 *Neuron* "Placebo analgesia: a predictive coding perspective"; Ongaro & Kaptchuk 2019 *Pain* "Symptom perception, placebo effects, and the Bayesian brain"; Tabor/Burr 2019; Hechler?) and say it is a LENS (C/B) — the same engine as lesson 03 (perception) — plus a pointer to 《计算心智》02.
- Social pain overlapping physical pain (Eisenberger 2003 / Woo 2014 non-overlap) — one grade-line only; likely C — cite the critique.
- Bet candidates: e.g. "Among adults with NO back pain who are 50 years old, what share show a bulging disc on MRI?" (Brinjikji); "share of people with a lower-limb amputation who report phantom limb pain"; pick-type: "does a clearly visible spinal 'abnormality' tell you…". Provide exact truths.
- Widget constants: published expectation effects on 0–100 pain ratings (cue high vs low), how large vs the nociceptive stimulus step; anything that gives a published stimulus–rating relation (Stevens power-law exponent for heat pain ≈ 1.0–2.0? - Boundary: expectation does not abolish pain; magnitude of placebo analgesia in clinical trials is small; imaging findings do not mean you can ignore symptoms; chronic pain mechanisms are heterogeneous; "pain is in the brain" is a slogan that misleads.

## 14 · 自控：做到自控的人，靠的不是硬扛 (≈120 bullets)
Story: the folk model of self-control = a muscle of willpower used to resist temptation. Evidence: (1) the muscle version (ego depletion) failed large replications [already lesson 02, D]; the marshmallow-predicts-life story shrank [already lesson 16, C/D]; (2) people with high trait self-control seem NOT to resist more; they report fewer/weaker temptation conflicts, more good habits, and arrange situations — self-control is largely situation selection + habits; (3) pre-commitment/environment design works better than effort in the (limited) trials. Find out how well each leg holds.
- ★ Hofmann, Baumeister, Förster & Vohs 2012 *JPSP* 102:1318–1335 "Everyday temptations: an experience sampling study of desire, conflict, and self-control" (N=205 adults, ~7 days, ~7,827 reports: share of waking time with a desire, share of desires in conflict with goals, share resisted, how trait self-control relates to each); Hofmann, Vohs & Baumeister 2012 *Psych Sci* (what people desire, feel conflicted about, resist). Replications/reanalyses.
- ★ Milyavskaya & Inzlicht 2017 *SPPS* "What's so great about self-control?…" (N, design; trait self-control predicted goal attainment through LESS temptation not more effortful resistance?) — read exactly what they found; Milyavskaya, Berkman & De Ridder 2019 (the many faces of self-control); Imhoff/Schmidt/Gerstenberg 2014; later experience-sampling studies (Hofmann et al. 2014? Wenzel et al.; Gillebaart; Brandstätter; Stinson?). Grade B/C and say which are single studies.
- ★ Galla & Duckworth 2015 *JPSP* "More than resisting temptation: beneficial habits mediate the relationship between self-control and positive life outcomes" (studies, N; mediation by habits); Gillebaart & de Ridder 2015 *Soc Pers Psych Compass* "Effortless self-control"; Gillebaart 2018 *Front Psychol*; de Ridder et al. 2012 *PSPR* meta-analysis (102 studies, N, mean r with outcomes; which behaviours).
- ★ Duckworth, Gendler & Gross 2016 *PPS* "Situational strategies for self-control" (situation selection/modification/attention/reappraisal taxonomy); Duckworth, Milkman & Laibson 2018 *PSPI* "Beyond willpower: strategies for reducing failures of self-control" (the table of strategies and the evidence quality behind each — transcribe the review's own evidence statements); Hollands et al. 2019 *Cochrane* (altering availability/proximity of food, alcohol, tobacco: number of studies, direction, certainty); Wood/Neal (habits already in FACTS_T3b).
- ★ Precommitment & implementation intentions with real numbers: Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 *Adv Exp Soc Psychol* (94 studies, d = 0.65) and any newer meta-analysis (Wang et al. 2021?; Bieleke 2021?), Webb & Sheeran 2006 (intention–behaviour gap: medium-to-large intention change → small-to-medium behaviour change), Giné, Karlan & Zinman 2010 *AEJ:Applied* (CARES smoking commitment contract: N, quit rate difference at 6 and 12 months), Thaler & Benartzi 2004 *JPE* (SMarT: savings rate), Milkman 2014 temptation bundling (effect faded; later replication?). **Avoid Ariely & Wertenbroch 2002 (retracted Sept 2026) and all Wansink; flag any Ariely coauthored paper.**
- ★ Trait self-control and life outcomes: Moffitt et al. 2011 *PNAS* (Dunedin, N≈1,000 followed to 32; self-control gradient and health/wealth/crime; sibling comparison in the E-Risk/dizygotic sample); Tangney/Baumeister/Boone 2004 (Brief Self-Control Scale; N, GPA r); critiques (Duckworth/Taxer 2019? "Self-control and academic: …"; Sperber 2024 already in FACTS_T3b). Grade B; say what is correlational.
- Mischel's strategy results (the part of the marshmallow work that is NOT about prediction): Mischel & Ebbesen 1970 (visible vs hidden reward; N and waiting times), Mischel/Ebbesen/Zeiss 1972, Metcalfe & Mischel 1999 hot/cool; Kidd, Palmeri & Aslin 2013 (reliability of the environment; N=28 and the waiting-time contrast) + any replication. Do not redo Watts 2018/Sperber 2024 (FACTS_T3b §11) — point to them.
- Ego depletion: point to the existing STUDY (FACTS_T1 / lesson 02) and add ONLY post-2021 developments if any (e.g. 2022–2026 reviews, "moderate-limited" process-model claims, Vohs 2021 multi-site); do not redo.
- Willpower beliefs: Job, Dweck & Walton 2010 *Psych Sci* ("lay theories of willpower"; limited-resource theory) and the replication/critique (Francis & Job 2018? / Vosgerau? / Miller et al. 2012) — grade C probably.
- Temptation and attention/cognitive load (Shiv & Fedorikhin 1999) — only if verified and not Wansink-adjacent; otherwise skip.
- Bet candidates: e.g. "In an experience-sampling study of 205 adults, for what share of waking hours did people report having some desire?"; "of the desires reported, what share conflicted with a goal?"; "Did people high in trait self-control report MORE resisting or FEWER temptations?" (pick). Give exact [V] truths.
- Widget constants: published rates (desires per hour, share in conflict, share resisted, share enacted), strength scales; effect of situation changes (Hollands; Giné); any published "effort cost" parameter (none likely → 示意). A toy model: temptation arrivals × strength vs effortful resistance vs exposure reduction.
- Boundary: self-control failures do exist and matter (trait self-control still predicts outcomes); situation design is not available to everyone equally; "habits" evidence in humans (FACTS_T3b) is limited; effortful-control is real in tasks (Stroop-type), just not a depleting muscle in the strong form; "willpower" is not nothing: it is probably a weak-to-modest lever.
