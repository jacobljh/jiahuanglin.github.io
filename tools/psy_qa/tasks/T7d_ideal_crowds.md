## 19 · 理想型：说得出的偏好，和真正被吸引的人 (≈120 bullets)
Story: asked in advance, people state a list of what they want in a partner; when they meet real people, those stated preferences barely predict who they end up attracted to; the strongest predictors of attraction and of relationship quality are partner-specific (how this person treats me, perceived responsiveness, perceived similarity), not the trait list. This is the "what you say you want ≠ what scores" dissociation (sibling of wanting ≠ liking, lesson 13, and of confabulated reasons, lesson 23).

## 20 · 群体：什么时候一群人比一个人聪明，什么时候更笨 (≈130 bullets)
Story: aggregating many independent judgments cancels their noise (lesson 08) and often beats the average member and sometimes the best — but only if errors are independent and diverse; once members influence each other (conformity, cascades, social influence), groups lose that advantage; and groups interacting freely also lose output (brainstorming, social loafing) and information (hidden profiles). So "wisdom" is a property of the aggregation rule, not of the number of heads.
- Already in FACTS (do not redo): Galton 1907 (787 estimates, median 1,207 lb vs 1,198 lb), Mellers 2014 (Good Judgment Project: teams > crowd-belief > individuals), the squared-error identity (error of the average = average error − diversity), conformity/Asch/Milgram/threshold model → FACTS_T4a §12 (point; do not redo).
- ★ Social influence undermines the crowd: Lorenz, Rauhut, Schweitzer & Helbing 2011 *PNAS* 108:9020–9025 (N=144 in 12 groups? what they found: diversity loss, "range reduction", "confidence effect"); and the counter-evidence: Becker, Brackbill & Centola 2017 *PNAS* 114:E5070 (N≈1,360; decentralised networks improved accuracy; centralised ones didn't) and Becker, Porter & Centola 2019 *PNAS* ("The wisdom of partisan crowds"); Davis-Stober et al. 2014 *Decision* (when is a crowd wise?); replication/critique status of Lorenz 2011 (e.g., Mavrodiev? 2013; Jayles 2017). Grade B/C honestly.
- ★ Larrick & Soll 2006 *Management Science* "Intuitions about combining opinions: misappreciation of the averaging principle" (bracketing; the studies; numbers: how often people choose the best judge/expect the average to be worse than the best); Soll & Larrick 2009 (strategies for revising judgment); Mannes/Soll/Larrick 2014 *JPSP* ("The wisdom of select crowds"); grade A/B.
- ★ Diversity & the diversity prediction theorem: Page 2007 *The Difference* / Hong & Page 2004 *PNAS* ("diversity trumps ability") AND the critique Thompson 2014 *Notices of the AMS* ("Does diversity trump ability?") — grade the theorem vs the "trumps ability" claim; Condorcet jury theorem and the correlated-errors caveat (Ladha 1992 *AJPS* / Dietrich?) — cite as [def]/derivation.
- ★ Information cascades & social influence in markets of opinion: Bikhchandani, Hirshleifer & Welch 1992 *JPE* ([def] model); Anderson & Holt 1997 *AER* (lab cascades: N, share of cascades); Salganik, Dodds & Watts 2006 *Science* 311:854–856 (N=14,341; 8 social-influence worlds vs independent; inequality/unpredictability; "quality" only partly predicted success) and replications (Krumme et al. 2012 *PNAS*; Abeliuk 2015?); Muchnik, Aral & Taylor 2013 *Science* (positive herding: +25% odds of further up-votes; N=101,281 comments; negative herding corrected). - ★ Brainstorming: Diehl & Stroebe 1987 *JPSP* ("Productivity loss in brainstorming groups: toward the solution of a riddle" — production blocking; N), Mullen, Johnson & Salas 1991 *Basic Appl Soc Psychol* (meta-analysis; nominal vs real groups: effect size), Paulus/Dzindolet/Poletes/Camacho 1993; Girotra, Terwiesch & Ulrich 2010 *Management Science* (individuals first then group: quantity and quality of best idea; N), electronic brainstorming (Dennis & Valacich 1993; DeRosa/Smith/Hantula 2007). Grade A/B (quantity), C for 'quality'.
