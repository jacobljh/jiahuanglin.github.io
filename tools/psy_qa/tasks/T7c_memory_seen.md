## 15 · 经历与记忆：你记得的，不是你经历的 (≈130 bullets)
Story: three ledgers of the same event — what you PREDICT it will feel like, what you actually EXPERIENCE moment by moment, what you REMEMBER — and the one that drives choice is usually the remembered/predicted one. Peak–end rule and duration neglect (graded by the latest meta-analysis), the "more pain preferred to less" experiment, and affective forecasting errors (impact bias, focalism) — each with its boundary conditions.
- ★ Kahneman, Fredrickson, Schreiber & Redelmeier 1993 *Psych Sci* 4:401–405 "When more pain is preferred to less: adding a better end" (cold-pressor; N; the two trials; the proportion who chose to repeat the longer trial; the ratings); replications (Schreiber & Kahneman 2000 aversive sounds; Ariely & Zauberman? — check authorship/retraction issues first; Stone/Broderick 2000; Kemp et al. 2008; Bornstein? ; any pre-registered replication).
- ★ Redelmeier & Kahneman 1996 *Pain* 66:3–8 (colonoscopy/lithotripsy: N=154 colonoscopy patients; real-time ratings every 60 s vs retrospective ratings; the correlations of retrospective ratings with peak/end average vs with duration); Redelmeier, Katz & Kahneman 2003 *Pain* 104:187–194 (randomised, N≈682; extra minute of milder discomfort at the end; the effect on retrospective rating and on the willingness/return for repeat colonoscopy — read the exact result; the result for the return rate is often misquoted); Kahneman 2000 "Evaluation by moments"; Fredrickson & Kahneman 1993 *JPSP* (film clips; duration neglect).
- ★ The latest meta-analysis: Alaybek, Dalal, Fyffe, Aitken, Zhou, Qu, Sander, McCloy? et al. 2022 *J Behav Decis Making* "All's well that ends (and peaks) well? A meta-analysis of the peak-end rule and duration neglect" — verify authors/venue; what it found for peak, end, mean (the beta/r), for duration neglect (supported? only when …), moderators (pleasant vs unpleasant, real vs lab, intensity measure). This decides the grades of the lesson's headline claims. Also any later (2023–2026) work (e.g. Kahneman's own caveats; Bartoshuk? ; "peak-end rule in real-world service evaluations").
- Real-world evidence: Do, Rupert & Wolford 2008 *Psychon Bull Rev* (pleasant), Rode/Cowen? (peak-end in clinic waits?), Miron-Shatz 2009 *Emotion* (multi-episode events; boundary conditions), Chajut et al. 2014 *Psych Sci* (labour pain recall), Wirtz/Kruger/Scollon/Diener 2003 *Psych Sci* (spring-break trips: online experience vs memory as predictors of wanting to repeat; N), Kahneman et al. 2004 *Science* Day Reconstruction Method (N=909; U-index) — each with the number and a grade; flag what is single-lab.
- Experienced vs decision vs remembered utility: Kahneman, Wakker & Sarin 1997 *QJE* "Back to Bentham?" ([def] + what they argue); Kahneman & Riis 2005; the "two selves" idea; critique (Dolan? Diener?).
- ★ Affective forecasting: Wilson & Gilbert 2005 *Curr Dir Psychol Sci* "Affective forecasting: knowing what to want"; Gilbert, Pinel, Wilson, Blumberg & Wheatley 1998 *JPSP* (durability bias; the tenure study: denied vs granted, N, happiness differences; the election/romantic-breakup studies); Dunn, Wilson & Gilbert 2003 *PSPB* (housing lottery); Wilson/Wheatley/Meyers/Gilbert/Axsom 2000 (focalism); Levine, Lench, Kaplan & Safer 2012 *JPSP* ("accuracy and artifact": when the impact bias shrinks); Wilson & Gilbert 2013 reply; Lench/Levine meta-analytic or review evidence ("focalism and the underestimation of future emotion" 2011; 2019 review) ; Hoerger 2012; the 2008 election study (Wilson 2000 / Levine 2012). Grade B (direction reliable; size depends on measure).
- Hedonic adaptation is already in lesson 13 §七 (FACTS_T3b §G) — point to it, do not redo.
- Duration neglect and medical decisions: do patients' remembered experience change later behaviour (return rates for colonoscopy, vaccination, dental)? Only if you can find a clean study.
- Myths: "memory of a bad ending always ruins the whole experience"; "duration doesn't matter at all" ([✗] if the meta-analysis says so); "peak-end is Kahneman's proven law" etc.
- Bet candidates: e.g. "In the cold-water experiment, what share of participants chose to repeat the LONGER (30 s warmer extension) trial?"; "In the randomised colonoscopy trial, adding a minute of milder discomfort … did the retrospective rating go up, down, or stay?" (pick); "share of the variance…" Give exact truths.
- Widget constants: published peak-end regression weights (peak, end, mean, duration) and their sizes from Redelmeier & Kahneman 1996 / Alaybek 2022; trial structures of the cold-pressor design (temperatures, seconds); the colonoscopy design (extra 60 s). Say which are published and which are 示意.
- Boundary: peak-end is a description of retrospective evaluation, not of decisions in general; ratings vs behaviour; the effect is weaker in long, multi-episode, or pleasant experiences (check); "experienced utility" itself is hard to measure.

## 18 · 别人眼里的你 (≈120 bullets)
Story: we judge ourselves from the inside (loud private evidence: my embarrassment, my slips, my anxious heartbeat) and others from the outside (quiet public evidence). The resulting errors are systematic: we overestimate how much others notice us (spotlight effect) and how much our inner states leak (illusion of transparency), and we underestimate how much others like us after a conversation (liking gap), how positively people respond to gratitude/kindness, and how willing they are to help when asked. Check each for replication, size and boundaries.
- ★ Gilovich, Medvec & Savitsky 2000 *JPSP* 78:211–222 "The spotlight effect in social judgment" (Barry Manilow T-shirt: the exact estimated vs actual proportions and N; other studies: group discussion, positive vs negative); replications (Many Labs 2 Klein et al. 2018 included the spotlight effect? verify; Ebersole 2016? ; other labs), cross-cultural data; grade.
- ★ Gilovich, Savitsky & Medvec 1998 *JPSP* 75:332–346 "The illusion of transparency: biased assessments of others' ability to read one's emotional states" (liars/ tasting task: estimated vs actual detection rates; N); Savitsky & Gilovich 2003 *JESP* (public speaking: telling speakers about the illusion reduces anxiety? N); Savitsky, Epley & Gilovich 2001 *JPSP* (overestimating the impact of our failures); replications (Many Labs? Savitsky/Gilovich later? Vorauer; Gilovich & Savitsky 1999 *CDPS*); grade (C/B).
- ★ Boothby, Cooney, Sandstrom & Clark 2018 *Psych Sci* 29:1742–1756 "The liking gap in conversations: do people like us more than we think?" (studies 1–5: lab conversations N, a field study (workshop), dorm/college residents; the size of the gap; how long it lasts); replications / cross-cultural / later work (Wolf? 2022; Mastroianni et al. 2021 *PNAS* "Do conversations end when people want them to?" — N, "in X% of conversations …" verify); Kardas, Kumar & Epley 2022 *JPSP* "Overly shallow? Miscalibrated expectations create a barrier to deeper conversation" (N; deep vs shallow; expectation vs experience); grade B.
- ★ Kumar & Epley 2018 *Psych Sci* "Undervaluing gratitude: expressers misunderstand the consequences of showing appreciation" (gratitude letters; N; senders underestimated how surprised/positive recipients felt, overestimated awkwardness); Kumar & Epley 2023 (kindness: "A little good goes an unexpectedly long way", JPSP? verify); Kumar & Epley 2021 *JEP:G* (compliments?) ; Zhao & Epley 2022 *Psych Sci* "Surprisingly happy to have helped: underestimating prosociality creates a misplaced barrier to asking for help" (N); Flynn & Lake 2008 *JPSP* "If you need help, just ask: underestimating compliance with direct requests for help" (N; predicted vs actual compliance; the factor); Bohns 2016 *CDPS* review "Underestimating others' willingness to help / compliance"; any replication or meta-analytic summary; grade B.
- Egocentric mechanism: anchoring on one's own perspective with insufficient adjustment — Epley & Gilovich 2001 *Psych Sci*, 2006 (anchoring-and-adjustment; grade given in FACTS_T1 for anchoring: DON'T re-grade, point), Epley, Keysar, Van Boven & Gilovich 2004 *JPSP* (perspective taking as egocentric anchoring); Keysar, Barr, Balin & Brauner 2000 *Psych Sci*; the "different information" account (inside view loud / outside view quiet). State clearly what is mechanism-claim (C) vs the descriptive effect.
- Pluralistic ignorance (Prentice & Miller 1993 *JPSP* campus alcohol norms — N, the estimates; Miller & McFarland 1987) — descriptive; later norms-feedback interventions have mixed evidence (Borsari & Carey 2003 meta-analysis? Foxcroft?) — only if verified; flag as C where interventions are concerned.
- Insight into how others see us (self–other agreement): Vazire 2010 *JPSP* (SOKA model; observers beat self for visible traits), Carlson & Furr; Kenny/ Kenny & DePaulo 1993 ("Do people know how others view them? An empirical and theoretical account", *Psych Bull*: people know the general impression others hold but little about specific others' views — "meta-accuracy") — verify numbers; grade B.
- Social anxiety is a related clinical area — do NOT go into it; one pointer sentence only if a source exists (lesson 25).
- Myths: "everyone is watching you"; "people think about you as much as you do"; "being honest about nerves always makes it obvious" — each with what the data say; and the corrections: the effect is on average and some settings (e.g., very conspicuous clothing) reverse it? — check.
- Bet candidates: e.g. "Students wore an embarrassing T-shirt into a room; what share of observers did they predict would remember it vs the actual?" (num); "after a first conversation, do people think their partner liked them MORE or LESS than the partner reports?" (pick); "strangers asked directly for help: actual compliance vs requesters' predicted compliance (Flynn & Lake)". Give exact truths.
- Widget constants: Barry Manilow figures; the liking-gap magnitude on the rating scale and the way it was measured; Flynn & Lake 2008 predicted vs actual compliance numbers; Kumar & Epley effect sizes (d). A toy "inside-view vs outside-view" model has no published parameters → 示意.
- Boundary: the effects are averages over mostly Western, mostly student/online samples (WEIRD, lesson 22); the spotlight effect shrinks for truly noticeable things; liking gap smaller/vanishes in some groups (check shy/anxious people) ; individual differences; effect sizes of "underestimation" depend on measurement.
