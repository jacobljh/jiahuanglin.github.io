Measure in the right unit

"Ten thousand hours" and "ten billion tokens" and "forty terabytes" can all describe the same corpus, and they rank corpora differently. One of the four available units is the one you actually care about, it is the smallest by about eight orders of magnitude, and almost nobody reports it.

Where we are
Lesson 00 asserted both rankings with a unit already assumed. Before ranking anything we should know what we are counting, because the choice of unit silently encodes a claim about what matters.

1 · Four units, one hour of video

Take one hour of 256×256 RGB at 8 fps — the standard world-model sampling plan — and count it four ways.

Bytes. The physical storage:

256 × 256 × 3 = 196,608 B/frame  ·  × 8 fps × 3600 = 5.7 GB/hour raw

Tokens. What the model actually processes, at patch 16:

(256/16)² = 256 tok/frame  ·  × 8 × 3600 = 7.37 × 10⁶ tokens/hour

Hours. One. This is the unit datasets are advertised in, and it is the only one that does not depend on the sampling plan — which is both its virtue and its uselessness for cost estimation.

Decision-relevant bits. Now the one that matters. Ask how many bits per second genuinely change what the robot should do. In our running insertion task: the gripper's offset from the socket in three axes at a resolution that matters (~1 mm over ~100 mm, so ~7 bits each), the contact state (2 bits), and a phase label (2 bits). Call it 25 bits per decision, at 8 decisions per second:

25 × 8 × 3600 ≈ 7.2 × 10⁵ bits/hour ≈ 90 kB/hour
Raw bytes5.7 × 10⁹
Tokens7.4 × 10⁶
Decision-relevant bits7.2 × 10⁵
Ratio, bytes to decision bits≈ 8,000 : 1

Roughly four orders of magnitude between what you store and what changes a decision — and that is with a generous 25-bit-per-decision estimate. This is the same fact track 1 lesson 02 derived from the loss side, arriving here from the accounting side: almost all of an embodied dataset is irrelevant, and it is the irrelevant part you pay to store, move, and attend over.

The fourth readout is the number to remember: the decision-relevant share of a token budget is a fraction of one percent. Not a rounding error to be optimised later — the dominant fact about the medium. And notice what happens when you change the patch size: tokens move by 16× while decision bits do not move at all, which means the token count is a property of your engineering choices and the decision bits are a property of the task. Only one of those is a fact about the world.

2 · Which unit to use for which question

questionunitwhy
Will this fit on disk? What does egress cost?bytesStorage and network bill in bytes and nothing else.
What will training cost in FLOPs?tokensCompute is 6·N·T; T is tokens. Lesson 12 adds the decode bill, which is in bytes.
How much human or robot time was this?hoursThe only unit proportional to acquisition cost, which is why collection is planned in it.
Will this teach the model the thing I need?decision bitsThe only unit that measures usefulness rather than quantity.
Is this corpus bigger than that one?ill-posedPick one of the four first; the orderings differ.
The substitution that causes the most confusion
Papers report hours (a proxy for acquisition cost) and blog posts report tokens (a proxy for compute cost), and readers compare them as if either measured usefulness. Neither does. DROID's 350 hours and a 15-trillion-token text corpus differ by ~5,700× in tokens and by an unbounded factor in what they teach a robot about contact. The comparison is not close in either direction, and which direction depends entirely on the unit — so state the unit, always.

3 · Two corrections that change the ordering

Even within a single unit, raw counts overstate what you have, for two reasons that both deserve reporting.

Effective, not raw. An hour of video of a static scene has the byte count of an hour and the information content of one frame. Near-duplicate demonstrations — an operator repeating a task 500 times — likewise. So report the effective count: unique clusters, or hours after de-duplication. Lesson 11 develops this; the point here is that it is a unit correction, not a data-cleaning detail, and a dataset's headline number is almost always the uncorrected one.

Per-capability, not global. A corpus has a different effective size for each thing you want to learn. Ego4D's 3,670 hours is 3,670 hours of semantics, a few hundred hours of manipulation-relevant hand-object interaction, and zero hours of contact force. One corpus, three sizes, and only the last two are the ones that gate the frontier.

size(corpus, capability) — not size(corpus)

That notation is the actual conclusion of the lesson. Corpus size is not a scalar. It is a function of what you are trying to learn, and lesson 02 turns that function into a price.

Takeaway
One hour of 256²/8 fps video is 5.7 GB, 7.4 M tokens, one hour, and about 7.2 × 10⁵ decision-relevant bits — roughly 8,000:1 from bytes to decision bits, so the decision-relevant share of a token budget is a fraction of one percent. Bytes answer storage, tokens answer compute, hours answer acquisition cost, and decision bits are the only unit that measures usefulness. Crucially, token count is a property of your engineering choices (change the patch size and it moves 16×) while decision bits are a property of the task. And correct twice before comparing: report effective rather than raw counts, and remember that corpus size is a function of the capability, not a scalar.

Where this points next

We now have a unit for usefulness. Combine it with the acquisition cost each source implies and every source can be placed on one axis: dollars per useful hour, for a named capability. Lesson 02 builds that ledger, and it produces a spread of about six orders of magnitude with a clear and uncomfortable structure — the capabilities that gate deployment have no cheap source at all.

  • Why is token count not a fact about the world? It depends on the patch size and frame rate you chose, moving 16× with a patch-size change, whereas the decision-relevant bits are fixed by the task. (§1)
  • Match each unit to the question it answers. Bytes for storage and egress, tokens for training FLOPs, hours for acquisition cost, decision bits for usefulness — and "is this corpus bigger" is ill-posed until a unit is named. (§2)
  • Why is comparing DROID's hours to a text corpus's tokens misleading in both directions? They differ by ~5,700× in tokens and by an unbounded factor in what they teach about contact, so the answer flips with the unit — the unit must be stated. (§2)

  • Explain why Ego4D has three different sizes. 3,670 hours for semantics, a few hundred hours of manipulation-relevant hand-object interaction, and zero hours of contact force — size is a function of the capability. (§3)
