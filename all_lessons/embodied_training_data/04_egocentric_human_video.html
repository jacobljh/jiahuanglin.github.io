Egocentric human video

Move the camera from the tripod to the forehead and two of lesson 03's biases disappear at once: the framing becomes a body's own view, and the camera's motion becomes a partial record of what the body did. That makes egocentric video the closest thing to action-labeled data that nobody had to instrument — and it is three orders of magnitude smaller than the ocean.

Where we are
Lesson 03's surviving web video was third-person, edited and photogenic. Two of those defects are properties of where the camera sits. This lesson takes the corpus that fixes them, states its real sizes, and corrects a number the literature routinely reports wrongly.

1 · Why the camera's position changes the data's category

Three consequences follow from mounting the camera on the actor, and the second is the one that matters most.

1 · framing
The robot's own viewpoint
Hands enter from the bottom of the frame at close range, objects are at manipulation distance, and the view is uncomposed. This is structurally like a head- or wrist-mounted robot camera.
2 · camera motion is body motion
A partial action record
Head and torso movement are directly recoverable from the video via visual odometry. So part of the action column is in the pixels — which is exactly the inverse-dynamics argument of track 1 lesson 06, available for free.
3 · unedited, long-form
Continuous dynamics
Recorded as continuous captures — Ego-Exo4D's takes run 1 to 42 minutes — so lesson 03's shot-boundary problem largely does not arise.

The second point deserves emphasis because it is a genuine free lunch. Third-person video contains the actor's motion only as observed geometry; egocentric video contains the wearer's motion as the camera trajectory itself, recoverable by standard structure-from-motion. You still do not get hand joint commands, but you get the base and head motion of the body doing the task, at no collection cost.

The 1,286 versus 221 correction
Ego-Exo4D is very often cited as "1,286 hours of egocentric video." It is 1,286 hours of video, counting every synchronised camera in a multi-camera rig, of which about 221 hours are egocentric. The exocentric hours are valuable for other purposes — they are the whole point of the paired design, enabling ego-exo correspondence learning — but they are not egocentric hours, and a plan that budgets 1,286 is off by a factor of six. Lesson 01's rule applies: count the hours of the view you actually need.

And put the scale in perspective. Ego4D's 3,670 hours is about ten times all of DROID and about 0.0004% of the web-video ocean. It sits in the awkward middle of the ledger: far too small to be treated as unlimited, far too large to ignore, and — critically — it has no action column in any robot's units and no forces at all.

Set the interaction fraction to a realistic 20–35% and the manipulation-relevant slice of Ego4D lands somewhere between 700 and 1,300 hours — two to four times all public robot data, from a corpus collected for entirely unrelated research purposes. That multiple is the reason egocentric video is worth the retargeting difficulty despite being small by web standards.

3 · What it is actually good for, and what it will never supply

Track 2 lesson 07 derived the per-capability transfer profile; the data-side summary is:

Affordance — where on an object a human chooses to actexcellent, and dense
Task structure — the ordering of steps in real long-horizon activityexcellent; takes run to 42 min
Object dynamics at manipulation distance and resolutiongood
Head and base motion, via visual odometrygood — the free partial action column
Hand pose, via hand trackingavailable; retargeting to a gripper is the hard part
Joint commands in a robot's unitsabsent
Contact forcesabsent, at any scale

One property of egocentric data is worth calling out as uniquely valuable and rarely exploited: it contains real failures. Nobody edits an Ego4D capture to remove the moment a jar lid slipped or an object was fumbled. Lesson 03 showed web video is systematically stripped of failure by the publication process; a continuous unedited egocentric capture is not. Given that track 2 lesson 02's theorem says recovery behaviour is exactly what you need, an unedited human corpus containing genuine fumbles and corrections is a resource whose value is well above its hour count.

A practical note on the exocentric half
Do not discard Ego-Exo4D's 1,065 non-egocentric hours as padding. Synchronised ego and exo views of the same action are a supervision signal you cannot get any other way: they let you learn the mapping between "what a task looks like from outside" (which is how most instruction and most third-person robot cameras see it) and "what it looks like from the actor's eyes" (which is how a wrist camera sees it). That correspondence is precisely the retargeting problem's perceptual half, and this is the only corpus that hands it to you labelled.
Takeaway
Mounting the camera on the actor fixes two of web video's biases at once: the framing becomes a body's own view, and camera motion is body motion, so part of the action column is recoverable from pixels by visual odometry — a free partial handle. Real sizes: Ego4D is 3,670 hours; Ego-Exo4D is 1,286 hours of video but only about 221 egocentric hours, so budgeting the headline is off by six. At a realistic 20–35% hand-object interaction rate, Ego4D's manipulation-relevant slice is two to four times all public robot data. It supplies affordance, task structure, dynamics and head motion; it never supplies joint commands or forces. And its underrated asset is that, being unedited, it contains real failures — the recovery behaviour web video is stripped of.

Where this points next

Everything so far is observation, in ever more useful framings, and none of it has an action column in your body's units or a single newton of force. The next source has both, in unlimited quantity, at almost no marginal cost — and gets exactly those two columns from the least trustworthy part of its own machinery. Lesson 05 prices simulation properly, and finds that its real cost is not the one people budget for.
