Action labels are the currency

Across every source in the ledger, one column decides whether abundance is useful: whether the data records what was done. So there are exactly two ways to obtain a grounded hour — pay a person to produce one, or pay for a few and use them to label an ocean — and the second is cheaper by a factor you can compute.

Where we are
Lesson 06 counted the entire real robot corpus at 10³–10⁴ hours. Lessons 03–05 counted the observation corpora at 10⁶–10⁹. The difference between them is one column. Track 1 lesson 06 derived why that column is recoverable; this lesson prices the two routes against each other and finds the crossover.

1 · Why the action is the currency, stated once

Two files, same size, same resolution, same hour of the same kitchen. One is video. The other is video plus a small column recording what the hands were commanded to do at each instant. That column is perhaps fifty bits per step against seven million tokens an hour — a rounding error in the file — and it is the entire difference between an hour that can teach a robot to act and an hour that cannot. Anything that scarce, that small, and that decisive is not a field. It is money.

An action is the minimal information that makes the next observation predictable from the current one:

at ≈ argmina H(ot+1 | ot, a)

Two things follow, and they are the two halves of this whole track's economics. Because the action is minimal, it is a tiny number of bits — a 7-dimensional end-effector command at modest precision is on the order of 50 bits, against an hour of video's 7.4 million tokens. And because it is what makes the transition predictable, those 50 bits per step are the difference between an hour that can teach a policy and an hour that cannot.

The ratio that defines the market
The action column is roughly 10⁻⁵ of the data volume and carries essentially all of the control-relevant information. So the price per bit of the action column exceeds the price per bit of the observation column by an enormous factor — which is the precise sense in which action labels are a currency rather than just another field. They are the scarce, high-value, denominated thing that everything else is traded for.

2 · Route one: buy grounded hours directly

Track 2 lesson 04's arithmetic: a teleoperator produces about 60 episodes per hour, and at a loaded ~$45 per interaction-hour the price of a grounded hour is simply

cost per grounded hour ≈ $45, flat, with no economies of scale

Flat is the important word. There is no volume discount, no amortisation, and no learning curve beyond modest process improvement. Ten thousand grounded hours costs ten thousand times one grounded hour. That linearity is what makes the second route worth computing.

3 · Route two: buy a few labels, label the ocean

Buy L labeled hours, train an inverse dynamics model on them, and pseudo-label U unlabeled hours you already have for free. Pseudo-labels are noisy, so price them as a channel — the useful fraction surviving accuracy α over a vocabulary of size |A|:

η = 1 − H(ε)/log₂|A|,    ε = 1 − α

The total grounded-equivalent hours and their unit cost are then

G(L) = L + U·η(α(L))   ·   $/grounded hour = ( L·cteleop + CIDM ) / G(L)

where CIDM is the fixed engineering cost of building and validating the model. Note the shape: the numerator grows linearly in L while the denominator grows with U·η, and η saturates — so the unit cost falls, bottoms out, and rises again. There is an optimal number of labeled hours to buy.

The canonical instance: OpenAI's VPT bought a contractor set on the order of two thousand hours (reported at about $2,000 of collection) and pseudo-labeled 70,000 hours of ordinary online video — a 35× multiplication in hours, and a far larger one in dollars per grounded hour.

Two readings worth extracting. First, the saving is large — one to two orders of magnitude at a VPT-scale ocean — which is why this technique reshaped the field rather than merely improving it. Second, and more useful for planning: the value of the second route is set by the size of the ocean you already have. Drag the corpus down to a thousand hours and the fixed engineering cost never amortises; the crossover is a property of your unlabeled holdings, not of the method. So "should we build an IDM?" is answered by counting your unlabeled corpus, before any modelling discussion.

4 · What the currency cannot buy

Three limits, and each one sends you to a different later lesson.

Actions with no visible consequence — grip force under a static holdlesson 08
Actions at states your policy visits, which no corpus contains in advancelesson 09
And the failure that produces confidently wrong physicssystematic IDM bias

That second row is the one that connects to the next lesson and it is worth being precise about. An inverse dynamics model recovers the action from its consequence. When there is no consequence in the observation — the hand squeezing harder on an already-held object changes nothing visible — there is nothing to recover from, so the label is unrecoverable in principle. And the actions that are invisible in this way are disproportionately the contact actions that decide manipulation tasks. The currency works for the actions you can see the effects of, and those are the easy ones.

The circularity to guard against
Pseudo-labels are model output. If they train a world model whose rollouts then retrain the IDM, the two converge on a shared error with no external signal to detect it — both models agree, and both are wrong. Keep a locked holdout whose actions were physically measured, evaluate the IDM against it stratified by situation (aggregate accuracy hides the systematic failure), and never let generated data into it. Track 1 lesson 06 §5 and lesson 11 of this track both develop the general form of this hazard.
Takeaway
The action is the minimal information making the next observation predictable — perhaps 50 bits per step against 7.4 M tokens per hour of video, so roughly 10⁻⁵ of the volume carrying essentially all the control-relevant information. That is the precise sense in which it is a currency. Route one, direct teleoperation, is $45 per grounded hour, flat, with no economies of scale. Route two — buy L labels, train an IDM, pseudo-label U hours at channel efficiency η — has unit cost (L·c+C)/(L+Uη), which has an interior minimum; VPT's ~2k contractor hours unlocked 70,000, a 35× multiplication. Crucially, the second route's value is set by the size of the ocean you already hold, so count your unlabeled corpus before discussing the method. And it cannot recover actions with no visible consequence — which are disproportionately the contact actions that matter.

Where this points next

The last row of §4 is the boundary of everything discussed so far. No labelling technique manufactures a quantity that is absent from the medium, and force is absent from every camera. Lesson 08 states how little force data exists, and then computes the one genuinely good piece of news in this whole ledger: unlike every other row, contact data has a step function — one capital purchase, and every subsequent hour carries it free.
